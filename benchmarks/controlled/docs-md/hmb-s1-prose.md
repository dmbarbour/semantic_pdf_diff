# Harbour Monitoring Buoy: System Requirements

## 1 Scope

This document states the requirements for the harbour monitoring buoy. Each requirement is identified, stated and verified as below.

## 3 Requirements

3.1 Survival Temperature. The buoy hull shall withstand ambient temperatures from -42 °C to +58 °C without damage. Rationale: The buoy winters in an unheated yard and is deployed through the summer. Verification: Test.

3.2 Data Retention. In the event of a loss of main power, the data logger shall retain every logged record for at least 85 hours. Rationale: Field crews may take several days to reach a buoy after an outage. Verification: Test, Analysis.

3.3 Hull Colour. The buoy hull above the waterline shall be painted international orange. Rationale: Mariners must see the buoy by day against open water. Verification: Inspection.

3.4 Record Format. The data logger should store its records in a documented, non-proprietary file format. Rationale: Records outlive the software that wrote them. Verification: Inspection.

3.5 Remote Update. The logger firmware may be updated over the satellite link. Rationale: Visits to the buoy cost a boat day each. Verification: Demonstration.

3.6 Mooring Inspection. The operator will inspect the mooring chain every 11 months. Rationale: Chain wear is the leading cause of lost buoys. Verification: Inspection.

3.7 Transmit Interval. While the buoy is moored, the telemetry unit shall report its position and water temperature at intervals of no more than 30 minutes. Rationale: The harbour office plots positions in near real time. Verification: Demonstration.

3.8 Lamp Visibility. The navigation lamp shall be visible at a range of at least 4.4 nautical miles on a clear night. Rationale: The buoy marks a channel used at night. Verification: Analysis.
