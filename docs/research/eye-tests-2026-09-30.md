# Eye tests for vision models

- **Date:** 2026-09-30
- **Asked by:** the owner. "read this line of random numbers at various sizes, like an eye test for humans"; binding "in a few forms (name=val, table rows, boxes with text connected by edges with text; generated graphs or charts...)"; "known good answers that cannot be guessed in context would be very useful for diagnostics".
- **Why:** hosts don't say how they resize images. DeepInfra ignores gemma-4's image budget (see [how gemma-4 sees images](gemma4-images-2026-09-30.md)), and prices and token counts can't be counted on. So what a model can read is measured by what it reads.
- **Code:** `src/semantic_pdf_diff/eyetest.py`; run with `scripts/eye_test.py`.
- **Results:** `benchmarks/eyetest/results.json` and `report.html`. Every answer is in `benchmarks/eyetest/replay.zip`, so the report is rebuilt offline.
- **Spend:** $0.31 for six models, 215 cards each.

## Method

- **Cards:** random codes and numbers (`HX-402`, `K7Q`, `0.075`, `±0.05`, `2'-9 1/2"`), drawn with PyMuPDF at known cap heights. Heights are in the image's own pixels, before the host resizes it.
  - Each image's text layer is checked against its answers before it's sent.
  - The letters leave out I, O and Q, as drawings do.
- **Families:**

| Family | What's asked | Scored |
|---|---|---|
| read | Transcribe 5 lines of codes and numbers; 8 glyph heights (4–16 px) × 9 image sizes and shapes × 2 seeds, plus Courier and Times | Share of items read exactly, in order; character error rate |
| pairs | Every name with its value: on one row, across a wide gap, or a value below its name | Right, misbound (another name's value), misread |
| table | 8 cells looked up by row and column label; 8 or 20 rows, with or without grid lines | Right, misbound (another cell's value: same row or column?), misread, declined |
| graph | Every arrow between boxes: start, end, label | Right, reversed, label misbound or misread, ends wrong, spurious |
| chart-values | Grouped bars with a legend, each value printed above its bar; values looked up by category and series | As tables |
| chart-axis | Bars read against a labelled axis, to the nearest half step | Within a quarter step |

- **Thresholds:** for each image size, the glyph height at which 90% of items are read. It's interpolated on a monotone fit, since reading can only get easier as glyphs grow.
  - One misread at one size (Qwen read `'` as `°` at 10 px) otherwise moved a threshold by 4 px.
- **Failed answers count as unread:** Mistral and gemma-3 sometimes looped until their output was cut off, on images whose text they couldn't read.
- **Models** (all at DeepInfra, temperature 0, one sample each): gemma-4-31B-it, and its "turbo" serving; Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct; Mistral-Small-3.2-24B; Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E; gemma-3-27b-it.
  - No OCR models are listed at DeepInfra.
  - Llama-3.2-11B-Vision and devstral-2 aren't at DeepInfra; the owner has access to them elsewhere.

- **gemma-4 (either serving; they match to within a card):**
  - It reads the smallest text of any model, while an image is within its budget.
  - Past about 800 px a side, its threshold grows in proportion to the side: 6.5–7.7 px per 1000 px of side, from 768² to 2048². That's the fixed budget of about 645,000 pixels, found from behaviour alone. Tokens stay at 406, whatever the size.
  - In the frame it resizes to, it reads text 5–7 px tall.
- **Qwen3-VL reads about 6 px at every size,** with tokens growing with area: it sees images at their own resolution, so larger renders help it.
- **Llama-4-Scout and Mistral-Small** read 7–9 px, worse at 2048². Llama-4 tiles images, and its tokens stop growing at 2,608. Mistral's stop growing past 1536².
  - Mistral looped on the 3072 × 768 band at small text.
- **gemma-3 is fixed at 896²,** and reads bands poorly.
- **Fonts (gemma-4, 1024²):** Courier is easier than Helvetica (5 px: 0.73 against 0.41 of items), and Times harder (6 px: 0.60 against 0.71).

## Binding

Share right, all sizes:

| Family | gemma-4 | Qwen3-VL | Llama-4-Scout | Mistral-Small-3.2 | gemma-3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pairs | 0.64 | **0.96** | 0.66 | 0.64 | 0.40 |
| table | 0.58 | 0.54 | 0.29 | **0.81** | 0.17 |
| graph (arrows) | **0.87** | 0.86 | 0.37 | 0.44 | 0.13 |
| chart-values | **1.00** | 0.81 | 0.47 | 0.97 | 0.14 |
| chart-axis | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.88 | 1.00 | 0.68 |

- **gemma-4's pair losses are acuity, not binding:** none are misbound. It misread names at 2048² with 8 px text, which is about 3 px in its own frame.
- **Dense tables fail on binding while their text is legible.**
  - With 20 rows at 1024² and 8 px, gemma-4 got 3 of 8 cells right, though it reads 96% of items at that size.
  - Across all its table cards, 19 of its 20 misbound values came from the same row or column. It declined 21 lookups.
  - Mistral-Small is best at tables. Qwen declines often (37 of 128).
- **Arrow direction:** gemma-4's only arrow errors are reversals, 15 of 118 arrows. Most of Qwen's are too (12, and 4 with the wrong ends).
  - Mistral reverses half the arrows (59 of 118), and Llama-4 misreads them in every way.
- **Printed chart values:**
  - gemma-4 is perfect, and Mistral nearly so.
  - Qwen sometimes gives another bar's value (14 misbound).
  - Llama-4 often does, and once read a category code (`H-857E`) as a value.

## What this means for us (gemma-4 on DeepInfra)

- **Tiles:** 420-point tiles rendered at 1000 × 1000 are shrunk to about 768². They need text at least about 3 points tall (caps), roughly a 4-point font. Rendering them at 768² would read the same.
- **Bands, measured:** at 1000 × 250 px the threshold is 4.6 px over 612 points, or 2.8-point caps. At the budget's shape (1584 × 384) it's 5.6 px, or 2.2-point caps.
  - That's 1.3 × finer. The research note's pixel count had predicted 1.6 ×.
- **Whole-sheet overviews** (2048 px on the long side) need caps of about 16 px, which only titles have.
- **Dense tables need fewer rows per query,** more than larger renders.
- **Arrows (flow diagrams, P&IDs):** about one in eight of gemma-4's is reversed (15 of 118).
- **Per-task models:** Mistral-Small's tables (0.81 against 0.58) are the clearest case for choosing a model per task (plans index: model discovery). Real documents would have to confirm it in a round.

## Limits

- Clean renders only: no scans, blur, JPEG, rotation or line noise yet.
- One prompt wording per family, one sample per card, temperature 0.
- 1–2 seeds per cell, so single cells are noisy; the thresholds pool 16–48 items each.
- The graph layouts are simple grids, and the charts use one style.
