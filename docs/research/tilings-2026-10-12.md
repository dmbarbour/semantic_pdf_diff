# Tilings: candidates, calibration and defaults

- **Date:** 2026-10-10 to 2026-10-12.
- **Asked by:** the owner, 2026-10-08: "We probably need a whole plan just to improve tile selection and find/create examples that lead to such perverse outcomes." Then, 2026-10-09: "I'm tempted to push on tile selection a bit".
- **The model's questions, the owner, 2026-10-10:** "send the vision model itself a small version of the image, perhaps one to three small sketches of heuristic tile selection (the most promising), and asking which tiling is the best, e.g. with labeled translucent overlays. The model cannot easily read the shrunk down page, but can probably identify areas of interest from a zoomed out view. We can also measure the quality of those answers by injecting some known-bad tiling test cases." Then: "one shrunk-down image without tiling, plus one per candidate with that candidate's tiling. We could also include a calibration step that tests if a model is any good at this sort of question, tuned with known-bad cuts and pseudo-random candidate ordering. Your variant seems useful, too, but could include a mixed or nonsense option for classifying bad tiles."
- **From:** the tile selection plan, retired 2026-10-21 (the [plans index](../plans/README.md#retired) says how to read it from git). Sections here are numbered as its milestones were; code comments and the [lever index](../reviews/levers.md) cite them as "milestone N".
- **Decided:** [0029](../decisions/0029-tiles-chosen-by-measures.md). Each lever's mechanism and status are in the lever index; what was left open, in its ideas and the plans index's *Tile selection, further*.
- **Code:** the measures (`tiling.py`), the choice (`tilechoice.py`), refinement under another cut (`retiling.py`), region labels (`regionlabels.py`). The lab: `bench/tiling.py` and `bench/gallery.py` (`scripts/tiling.py`); the calibrations `bench/calibration.py`, `labelling.py` and `asking.py` (the scripts of the same names); the forced measure `bench/refinement.py` (`scripts/refinement.py`).
- **Where the data is:** the calibrations' profiles and answers are committed (`benchmarks/calibration/`, `labelling/`, `asking/`), their reports drawn again offline (`report`). The measures' runs, the gallery and the reads with the model stay local (`runs/`, `benchmarks/tiling/`), so the tables below are their record.
- **How to read the tables:** "today" is the defaults of the day, before milestone 8's. The controlled corpus is scored by its keys; real pages have no key, so they're compared by the distinct numeric values every task together found. One reading each, with gemma-4.
- **Spend with the model:** milestone 2, $0.030 and $0.001; 3, $0.307; 4, $0.460; 5, $0.502, then ties $0.345 and $0.014; 6, $0.151; 7, $1.24; 8, $1.56 ($1.03 of it a slices recording made before the decoration fix, whose answers no run now uses), and the corpus re-recorded, $0.042.

## 1. Measures without the model (2026-10-10)

`tiling.measure` measures any tiling of a page; its docstring lists the measures. It's in the product, not the lab, because tile choice ranks cuts by it at run time (milestone 5).

- **The trials review reproduced exactly** (the first 40 pages of each sample PDF, 803 pages):
  - grid: 244 pages, 6,582 tiles, 6,400 lines cut sideways, on 236 pages
  - bands: 559 pages, 822 tiles, none cut sideways
- **Every page of the samples** (4,157 pages):
  - **Grid pages are 14% of the samples' pages but 76% of their tiles.** They hold every line cut sideways (13,320, on 567 pages), and 93% of the lines read twice.
  - **Bands cut no line sideways, but 2,598 across:** a dense table with no gap between rows makes a band fall back to an overlapping cut (milestone 2).
- **Speed:** about 0.2 s a page, mostly PyMuPDF reading the text layer.

## 4. Calibration: can a model judge tilings? (2026-10-11)

The question is `tilechoice.py`'s. The cases, the arms and the pass mark (`calibration.PASS`, set by judgement: the known-good chosen in 90% of asks, and the same tiling in both orders in 85%) are `bench/calibration.py`'s. gemma-4 passes under both arms. The full profile, by kind, comes from `scripts/calibration.py report`.

- **Known-good found:** 126 of 146 pages. None on the scans or the plan sheets, whose figure is larger than a tile.
- **Questions:** each pairs the known-good with two bad kinds; 371 questions in all, each asked in two orders.
- **gemma-4, by arm** (742 asks each; dimmed also greys out what no tile holds, added after the first arm's misses):

  | | Outlined | Dimmed |
  |---|---|---|
  | known-good chosen | 90.2% | 95.1% |
  | the same tiling in both orders | 86.8% | 91.4% |
  | the known-good chosen in both orders | 83.6% | 90.8% |
  | known-good tilings given a problem | 12.1% | 6.1% |
  | invalid answers | 0 | 0 |
  | choices by position (A, B, C; the known-good at 33%, 35%, 32%) | 33%, 36%, 31% | 35%, 35%, 31% |

- **What it shows:**
  - **gemma-4 judges stark tilings well, with no position bias to speak of.**
  - **Outlines alone hide what a tiling misses.** A tiling missing a page's table, keeping only a tile around the page number, looked like the page untouched: chosen over the known-good in both orders on several table pages. Greying out what no tile holds took "missing" from 20% to 4%.
  - **The problems named are reliable for some faults only:** cut text, decoration and blank tiles (92 to 100%). Figure splits are named half the time; overlap almost never ("repeats text" 1%) though the overlapping tiling is rarely chosen. Tile choice checks only the reliable ones against the measures (`tilechoice.RELIABLE`).
  - **Complaints about known-good tilings are mostly "decoration or blank"** (38 of 46 named, dimmed). In 37 of those 38 asks, the tiling has a tile holding one short line, the page number: page furniture, though the measures count it as text.
- **Not shown:** drawing sheets and scans, which have no known-good tiling by these rules; faults less stark than these (milestone 5's near cases); pages beyond the corpus's few templates.
- **Cost:** about 1,300 input tokens per question (four images at about 260 tokens each, and the prompt), about $0.0002 with gemma-4.

## 6. The model labels regions (2026-10-12)

The lever and its rules are in the lever index's *Region labels*. The labels, the owner's "nonsense" and "other" among them (2026-10-10), are in `regionlabels.py`. The calibration's cases and its pass mark, revised after the run, are in `bench/labelling.py`.

- **Calibration** (gemma-4, $0.107; 271 questions on 146 pages, 1,428 regions, each asked numbered twice, the second time reversed; the full table from `scripts/labelling.py report`):
  - **Labels right:** 74.5% of 2,856 asks (the same in both numberings 80.7%).
  - **What the lever acts on holds:** content lost 0.8% (a table, chart, drawing, scan or mixed region called text, decoration or blank: 4 of 494), tiles saved 97.3% (text, decoration or blank called so).
  - **Nonsense isn't seen** (3.9%). As with the tiling question's "repeats text", the model judges what a region holds, not how it's cut. A half-figure is labelled by its kind and sent, and a strip of a paragraph as text.
  - **"Mixed"** was right 53.8% of the time; **"other"** was never said.

- **What it shows:**
  - **Another tiling, by the measures, is the better refinement:** fewer reads, more facts right on the corpus, and on real pages half the complaints, a third fewer quotes not in the page and half the claims bound to other words. On a grid page, 8 to 24 halves become 1 to 3 tiles of the XY-cut, whole panels of a slide (seen by eye).
  - **Asking adds nothing the measures don't know.** `said` hardly retiles (answers rarely say they're cut); `asked` re-asks every tile with a new rule, retiles a little more, and its misbound claims rise (38 → 49 on the corpus). A cut the model doesn't see is one the measures find anyway.
  - **Halves cut around figures:** loose claims from halves 93 → 51 on the corpus, and with `measures` 23. Measured without the model on 496 tiles: figures split by halves 40 → 20, lines cut unchanged, 27 tiles' halves moved.   - **A figure looked at again** gives fewer loose claims (93 → 49) and fewer right ones (431 → 412): told what it found, the model returns less. Not worth it over halves cut around the figure.
  - **No document's facts change** in any arm: the text tasks and tiles read them first. What refinement changes is the claims beside them.
  - **The old cut's wins aren't won back:** HabEx p4's band halves find nothing in any arm; on calg's sheet, `measures` reads 33 tasks for today's 66, complaints 3 → 0.
  - **Refinement is rare in a normal run** (8 refined tiles in the corpus). The forced measure is where these levers show; a normal run changes little.
- **Not built:** milestone 5's question among the other cuts (the asking calibration says the model's view adds little here), and labels guiding the cut (the detected figures give the parts' places; a label has none, and calibration found "mixed" 54% of the time). Both are ideas in the lever index.
