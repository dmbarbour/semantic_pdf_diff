## Results: neither decided ($1.04)

| Variant | Units changed | Judged | Win rate (90% interval) | By kind | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| details | 8 of 137 | 8, all | 0.64 (0.41–0.86) | visual only | inconclusive: too few units |
| references | 17 of 137 | 17, all | 0.50 (0.35–0.66) | text 0.47 (0.31–0.63), table 1.00 (1 unit) | inconclusive: neutral |

**details: the predicted win, with a resolution flaw.**
- **Wins, as predicted.** On dc-cd, values were bound to the right detail: Module A's 7'-0" centre segment and 12'-3 3/4" length, and beam openings per grid line. The judges on both orders said the baseline confused the modules and grid lines.
- **Losses, from resolution.** On calg p2 and p4 the variant lost the title block's revision table, which the baseline read.
  - The detail crops had been sized by a legibility rule, reaching about 620 points against the grid's 420.
  - gemma sees about 768 px, so the title block's 9 pt print dropped to about 11 px per em and the model read almost nothing there.
- **Fixed** (`71a46ae`): details keep their bounds and titles, but anything larger than a tile is tiled like a page. The image count is now about the grid's (28–38 per sheet against 35), with 1–23 lines cut instead of 23–79.
- **Lesson:** the "12 px per em" legibility rule from round 1 is too lax for gemma's effective resolution. Measure before trusting a rule for enlarging crops.

**references: no effect.**
- Most judged differences were run-to-run variation: fresh readings of the same chunk.
- **One attributable win:** on an NREL page, filter values were bound to the "generator speed low-pass filter" named in the cited Figure 7-1 caption.
- **One loss:** an IEA figure-caption chunk where the variant returned no claims at all; the cause is unclear.
- **Not accepted.** The interval's lower bound (0.35) fails the no-worse bar (0.45). It is cheap and rarely active (17 of 137 units), so it can be revisited with a larger sample if citations become a complaint.

## r05b: details again, with more drawing sheets

- Eight sheet pages are too few to decide, so the development set gains two slices of detail sheets:
  - dc-cd pages 31–38: 4–6 numbered details each
  - calg-cd pages 62–66: 3–6
- r05b judges the fixed `details` lever against the champion on the enlarged development set, up to 21 sheet units.
- **Prediction:** a win on sheet visuals of about 0.65–0.75. Losses, if any, would come from details whose titles aren't grid references and fall back to the grid (then unchanged).

### r05b results: neutral, with a second flaw ($1.36)

| Units changed | Judged | Win rate (90% interval) | By document |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 of 176 | 20, all | 0.50 (0.38–0.64) | dc p39–42 0.79 (3), calg p62–66 0.54 (5), dc p31–38 0.47 (8), calg p39–42 0.31 (4) |

- **Wins:** binding values to the right detail again.
  - dc: beam sections per detail, Module A and B dimensions, the crane attachment's dimensions
  - calg: glass-tile adhesive in the shower detail versus porcelain tile in the backsplash
- **Losses:** mostly title-block metadata the variant omitted (revision history, lot number, client, copyright), plus sheet notes.
- **Cause:** the side columns' tiles were now legible (tile 26 on calg p2 renders the revision table crisply), but gemma returned no claims for them. Their note said "Title block or notes column", and the model evidently took that as "no engineering content". The baseline's grid tiles, with no note, read 12 claims there.
- **Fixed** (`2d578be`): side columns are noted with the sheet only. r05c repeats the comparison; only the column tiles are re-asked.
- **Lesson:** a label in the context can switch extraction off. Context notes should describe where a crop is, not what kind of content to expect.

### r05c and r05d: details parked ($2.10)

| Round | Change | Units | Win rate (90% interval) |
|---|---|---|---|
| r05c | Side columns noted with the sheet's title only | 20 | 0.48 (0.34–0.61) |
| r05d | Side columns with no note at all | 21 | 0.42 (0.28–0.56) |

- **The title-block claims are still missing without a note.** On calg p2 the tile over the revision table returned nothing (the model: "only project administrative metadata… no engineering claims"). The baseline's grid tile over the same spot also took in part of a building section, and gemma extracted all 12 claims from it.
- **So gemma decides relevance from what the crop contains.** A crop holding only a title block reads as administrative; the same text beside a drawing reads as part of the sheet. Isolating regions cleanly, the point of the lever, is exactly what switches this off.
- **Per document (r05d):** dc p39–42 0.75 (the sheets the lever was designed on), dc p31–38 0.45, calg p39–42 0.31, calg p62–66 0.17. Beyond the title block, the calg losses are misbound elevation titles ("South Bathroom Elevation D5" for "North…"): the detail title in the note can be wrong when detail numbers sit close together.
- **Decision: parked.** `sheet_details` stays in the code, off by default. Across r05–r05d it cost about $4.50.

**Lessons, carried forward:**
1. **Crops change what the model thinks is in scope,** not only what it can see. Any cropping lever should be checked for dropped categories (metadata, notes), not just accuracy.
2. **A detail title attached from layout can be wrong** when details are dense. A wrong title in the context is worse than none.
3. **Open rubric question for the owner:** should title-block metadata (revision history, lot number, client, copyright) count as claims? The judges treat it as "critical engineering metadata" and penalize its absence. The extraction prompt asks for engineering claims, and for comparing designs revision dates matter less than values. If it shouldn't count, the judging prompt should say so. If it should, the extraction prompt should say so, instead of leaving it to how a crop happens to look.
