# Lever index

Every lever tried: in the [improvement rounds](../plans/query-improvement-2026-09-26.md) until they paused (2026-09-28), and since 2026-10-14 mostly on paired documents against null arms (see *Measurement notes*): what it does, what we expected, what happened, why we think it happened, and what to try next.

- **Detail:** the [rounds' record](../research/rounds.md). Each round's review was retired on 2026-10-21 (the [plans index](../plans/README.md#retired) says how to read one from git).
- **Per-unit clues:** `benchmarks/rounds/<round>/pairs-<variant>/analysis.json`, browsable as `insights.html` in the round folder (regenerate with `scripts/run_round.py <round> --only report`).
- **Win rates:** the variant's share of pairwise preferences against the baseline, judged in both orders, with a 90% interval (a bootstrap until 2026-09-28; since then cluster-robust, by page region).
- **This index is the lever catalogue** (the owner, 2026-09-28): every lever idea gets a row when it's raised, built or not. Levers not accepted or parked are tried again against later champions, and other ways of doing one idea are tried as its variants.

### Table structure (`table_structure`) and the PDF table heuristics
- **Mechanism:** a PDF table's lines go through heuristics (`tables.py`: rows joined across row boundaries without a drawn rule in a mostly ruled table; a two-line header merged; a stacked table, styled as the header, split off), then, for a part with a suspect line, the structure query (`tablestructure.py`): its lines tagged with signals, its image, rules from a closed vocabulary applied mechanically, examples copied from the image checked, the outcome reviewed. The design and its decisions: [0024](../decisions/0024-pdf-table-structure.md).
- **The owner (2026-10-07):** "Is there a good way to identify 'suspicious' lines for an image check on merging rows? ... Do we get no easily actionable feedback about where the table lines are drawn for a PDF?"; then "Ideally, we'd not provide the full table in bands for processing via vision, instead getting some comprehensible suggestions/rules about how to work around confusing formatting, with feedback similar to how we approach claims rules."
- **Expected:** the image check's structure failures and the misbinding they cause gone where rules are drawn; elsewhere, suspect lines settled by the model's rules. **Got** (not a round, 2026-10-07):
  - the corpus: true cells found 0.907 to 0.966 offline; image-check failures 254 of 394 to 114 of 408; misbound 229 to 209 (row by row 226 to 199); facts read right and the revision pairs as before; the structure query asked on 40 parts, nearly all floor plans and charts taken for tables
  - HabEx p4 (sc01 item 1) as printed, by the drawn rules alone; HabEx p7 by the structure query's rules
  - the slices: 15 of 96 parts restructured, 42 "not a table" (on the real reports, all charts), 19 failing twice
- **Parsers and repairs tried offline** (2026-10-07, Claude's benchmark, not committed: the corpus's 1,688 true cells, by row label, column label and value, found in the parsed grids): PyMuPDF as is 0.907; a two-line header merged 0.937; parts (`pdf_parts`: the header merged, stacked tables split; adopted) 0.966; parts with strategy `lines_strict` 0.961, with PyMuPDF's `refine` 0.951, with strategy `text` 0.212; the layout add-on's own table detection 0.057, its union with line detection 0.963; parts and rows joined across unruled boundaries (adopted) 0.966, 0.967 with cut columns joined. ### Skip empty tiles (`skip_empty`)
- **Mechanism:** tiles with no text line, drawing or image aren't sent; the rest keep their numbers, so recorded answers still replay.
- **Found:** in round 5d's analysis, the model's issue notes kept saying "the image is blank".
- **Measured without the model:** 43 of 963 champion tiles skipped on the development slices, and none of them had yielded a claim.
- **Accepted into the champion as a pure cost saving** (2026-09-27). There are no changed answers to judge.

### Quote matching (`quote_match`)
- **Found in the overall review:** the verbatim quote check dropped a third of all text claims (2,826 of 8,661 in recorded answers). They were mostly elided quotes, line-end hyphenation, Unicode variants, and table cells joined with "|".
- **`fragments`:**
  - normalizes Unicode, case, dashes, quote marks and line-end hyphens
  - accepts quotes whose "…" or "|" parts each appear, contiguous and in order
  - recovers about 1,070 claims
  - **Accepted in r09b:** 0.66, text 0.77. Missing fell sharply; misbound rose.
- **Post-mortem (2026-09-30, Gemini reading r09b's samples):** "…" may join distant fragments (separate axis labels, far-apart table cells), so it can verify a misbound claim. That fits misbound rising.
  - **Next:** cap how far "…" may reach (no crossing a blank line or another block), or accept "|" joins only in tables and "…" only in prose.
  - The weak visual partition in that batch (0.44) was mostly merged-claim wording shown in visual units (a bug since fixed), which the analyst couldn't know.
- **`excerpts`:** also accepts words read in order across a pseudo-table. It recovers about 1,700 claims, but let chart-axis labels through as values (r09: 0.51). Not accepted.
- **Measurement notes:**
  - No requests change, so testing costs judging only.
  - An A/A control in the same round (r09: 0.48 ± 0.12) gives the noise floor.

### Sheet details (`sheet_details`)
- **Mechanism:** a drawing sheet cut into its numbered details, each crop titled with the sheet and detail; side columns (title block, notes) read separately.
- **Got:** it won where designed (dc p39–42 0.75), and lost elsewhere.
- **Why, in three layers:**
  1. **Crops too large:** oversized crops lost small print (fixed).
  2. **A note switched extraction off:** "Title block" in the note made gemma skip a legible revision table as "not engineering".
  3. **An isolated crop reads as out of scope:** even untitled, a crop holding only a title block reads as administrative. Grid tiles mixing drawing and title block read it.

  Also: wrong detail titles where detail numbers sit close together.

### Refinement (`another_tiling`, `halve_parts`, `refine_figures`, `least_half`)

Built in [tile selection](../plans/README.md#retired) milestone 7, all off by default (`least_half` at 25%, as before). A partial tile's next reads come from `Platform.refined_tile`, given its answer.

- **First, the cut between lines** (2026-10-09, before these levers; [0029](../decisions/0029-tiles-chosen-by-measures.md)): a partial tile had been halved at its middle along its longer side, its text layer clipped with it, so a band was cut down every line. Now cut at whitespace nearest the middle of what the tile holds, each half grown to whole lines. Without the model, every band and grid tile of the samples' 803 pages refined once (7,365 tiles): lines cut by a tile's halves 3.08 → 0.05 on average, bands with 5 or more cut 584 → 13 of 783, empty halves 529 → 35 (839 when cut at the tile's own middle). Forced on the controlled corpus's 80 PDFs (321 tiles): right claims 1,870 → 2,153, loose 905 → 482, misbound 232 → 107, facts found right 2,002 → 2,371. Judged pairwise on 69 tiles, the new halves won: Gemini 3.1 Pro 37 to 10, Qwen3-VL 38 to 5. $0.57 measured, $3.56 judged.
- **The levers:**
  - `another_tiling` (`retiling.py`): the page's next candidate cut by the measures, its tiles holding what the partial tile cuts, each read once a page. Always (`measures`), where the answer says it's cut (`said`), or where it names the edges cutting content when a rule asks (`asked`). Halves where the measures find nothing cut.
  - `halve_parts`: halves cut around the detected figures the tile holds, where they can be.
  - `refine_figures: again`: a tile mostly one figure looked at again whole, told its claims and issues.
  - `least_half`: the least share of what a tile holds each half keeps.
- **Calibrated** (`scripts/asking.py`, gemma-4): asked to name a cut, it names one for 48.8% of tiles that cut content and 0.8% of whole ones; today's prompt, 31.2% and 10.5%. Neither passes (half the cut tiles).
- **Got** (every tile refined, `scripts/refinement.py`): `measures` with `halve_parts` is the best arm. On the controlled corpus, refined reads 197 → 135, their facts right 453 → 514, loose 93 → 23, misbound 38 → 24; whole documents' facts unchanged. On 16 real pages, refined reads 257 → 151, quotes not in the page 78 → 54, claims bound to other words 246 → 114.
- **Defaults since milestone 8** (2026-10-12): `another_tiling: measures` and `halve_parts`. A tile continued at the claim limit is halved, not retiled: read under the other cut, HabEx p403's dense table lost 43 of 454 values. Refinement is rare in a normal run, so the forced measure is where they show.

### Continued reading (`continuations`)
- **Mechanism:** an image task whose answer is incomplete with its claims at the limit, or says the claim limit stopped it ("more steps (17–22) than the 20 claim limit allowed"), is asked again ("<task>-c<n>"), the claims it returned listed by entity, attribute and value, not to be repeated; up to `continuations` times (3; lever `continue_reading`). That's the model asking for more; clipping or illegibility isn't. A first request's bytes are as before, so recorded answers replay.
- **The owner (2026-10-03),** on Claude's five ideas for diagrams with many claims (TS 38.300: 8 of 338 picture reads hit the 20-claim limit; a median picture had 95% of its label words in some claim, the largest procedure diagrams 76–100%):
  - continue when the model asks for more: "I think enabling 'continue' based on a request from the model is straightforward." Built.
  - labels no claim covers: "Analysis of labels unused is probably better applied as a quality and confidence check." Built as the label check, never a trigger ([how it works](../how-it-works.md#text-files-and-word-documents)).
  - a picture split in halves: "I think splitting halves will be very confusing in some cases; the purpose of the vision model is to see and report things as a human might, and visual context is a big part of that." Not built (2026-10-04).
  - the model proposes the partition: "Support for interactive analysis of images (4) would be somewhat analogous to progressive disclosure, but I agree it should be secondary." An idea below.
  - a picture's source read (Msc-generator's chart text, Visio's shape text): "Reading source for pictures is very nice to have as a fallback for attempting to understand claims." In *More formats* ([plans index](../plans/README.md#tentative)).

## Measurement notes (affect how to read the table)

- **Paired documents since 2026-10-14:** a lever is judged on the controlled corpus by `scripts/paired.py` ([judging a lever](../samples-and-testing.md#judging-a-lever)): arms share answers, each document's change is summed, with a sign-flip p-value, against null arms where a lever rewords many queries. Three do-nothing image rules compared pairwise called 7 of 83 measures a change at p < 0.1; facts right never moved by more than 6 of 3,409, while claim counts swung by up to 221.
- **A/A noise:** two readings of the same pages score about 0.5 ± 0.08 over 48 units (r01), so single units mean little.
- **Early stopping at 16–24 units inflates wins;** the combination rounds (r02, r04) at 40–48 units are more honest.
- **Before round 7,** units with more than 25 claims showed judges separately drawn samples, which added noise to visual units in every round. This was fixed in `5850dfd`.
- **Rubric v2 from round 7:** document administration is neutral; judges tag each side's problems and may leave remarks.
- **Free cost saving noticed in r05d's analysis:** the model kept reporting blank tiles. Built as `skip_empty` (see above).
